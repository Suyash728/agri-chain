4: Proposed Methodology
[151] (normal) The proposed methodology integrates the Internet of Things (IoT), Artificial Intelligence (AI), and blockchain technology to improve the transparency, reliability, and traceability of agricultural supply chains. The framework tracks agricultural products from farmers to consumers while validating the data collected during storage and transportation. It combines IoT-based monitoring, AI-driven telemetry validation, blockchain-supported record management, cloud-based storage, and a web-based interface to provide reliable supply chain information to different stakeholders.

[155] (normal) 4.1 System Overview
[156] (normal) The proposed framework consists of IoT-based data collection, an AI Trust Layer, a blockchain integration layer, cloud-based storage, and a user interface. These components work together to monitor agricultural products, validate telemetry data, maintain traceability records, and provide relevant information to supply chain stakeholders. The supply chain follows the movement of agricultural products from farmers to ware-houses or logistics providers, followed by dark stores or retailers, and finally to consumers. The AI Trust Layer evaluates incoming telemetry before it is considered for further processing. Blockchain supports the recording of product registration, custody transfers, and supply chain events, while the web interface provides role-specific access to relevant information.
[157] (normal) 4.2 Data Collection
[158] (normal) Environmental data, such as temperature and humidity, is collected through IoT sensors associated with the product monitoring system. GPS information supports location tracking and helps identify physically implausible movements during transportation. The system also uses simulated telemetry data to evaluate its behavior under normal and faulty Operating conditions. The telemetry simulator generates readings representing different supply chain scenarios, including normal environmental conditions, abnormal sensor values, unexpected location changes, timestamp inconsistencies, repeated events, and missing telemetry. These inputs support the evaluation of the data validation and anomaly detection mechanisms.
[159] (normal) 4.3 AI-Based Trust Layer
[160] (normal) The AI Trust Layer is developed using Python and FastAPI. It acts as a validation stage between telemetry data collection and the blockchain integration layer. Its primary objective is to identify unreliable or suspicious data before it is considered for supply chain records. The main processing steps are described below. 
[161] (normal) • Schema Validation: Checks whether incoming telemetry data contains the required fields and follows the expected data structure and format.
[162] (normal) • Physical and Temporal Plausibility: Examines sensor readings, timestamps, and location information to identify unrealistic values, excessive movement speeds, and inconsistent event sequences. 
[163] (normal) • Feature Extraction: Converts telemetry information into an 11-dimensional feature vector that represents relevant characteristics of the incoming data for anomaly detection. 
[164] (normal) • Anomaly Detection: Uses the Isolation Forest machine-learning algorithm to identify telemetry patterns that differ significantly from expected behavior. 
[165] (normal) • Integrity and Replay Checks: Applies SHA-256-based event fingerprinting to generate a fingerprint of telemetry events and uses replay detection to identify repeated records and suspicious event sequences.
[166] (normal) • Verdict Generation: Classifies incoming telemetry as VALID, ANOMALOUS, or INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE based on the results of the validation and detection stages.
[167] (normal) • Quarantine and Handoff: Routes suspicious data for quarantine and places data with insufficient supporting evidence on hold. Telemetry that satisfies the required validation conditions is prepared for handoff to the blockchain integration layer.

[171] (normal) 4.4 Blockchain-Based Traceability
[172] (normal) The blockchain component supports traceable records of agricultural product registration, custody transfers, and supply chain events. Smart contracts enforce predefined rules for relevant operations and help maintain consistent records across supply chain stakeholders. Product information and custody changes can be associated with the corresponding stages of the supply chain, allowing authorized participants to retrieve relevant traceability information. The AI Trust Layer acts as a validation gate before eligible telemetry is handed over to the blockchain integration layer. This separation allows AI-based data assessment and blockchain-based record management to perform distinct roles within the overall framework.
[173] (normal) 4.5 Data Storage and User Interface
[174] (normal) The framework incorporates cloud-based storage for supporting application data and document storage for relevant product information. These storage components complement the blockchain layer by supporting the management and retrieval of application records and associated documents.
[175] (normal) A web-based interface provides role-specific functionality for farmers, logistics providers, dark stores or retailers, and consumers. Supply chain participants can access information relevant to their roles, while the consumer interface provides product traceability information through QR-based access. By connecting product records with the product journey, the interface improves visibility into the movement of agricultural goods from their origin to the final consumer.
[176] (normal) 4.6 Testing and Evaluation
[177] (normal) The AI Trust Layer is evaluated using automated tests and simulated telemetry scenarios. The simulator generates normal readings and faulty cases, including temperature and humidity spikes, GPS jumps, out-of-order timestamps, replayed events, telemetry gaps, and combinations of these conditions. The evaluation examines the behavior of schema validation, physical and temporal plausibility checks, anomaly detection, integrity checks, verdict generation, and quarantine handling. Automated tests verify the expected behavior of the implemented validation components, while simulated scenarios provide controlled inputs for examining the system’s ability to identify suspicious telemetry. The recorded automated test suite contains 143 passing tests. In a separate simulated evaluation involving 400 telemetry transmissions, the system detected all 350 injected faulty cases and correctly classified 33 of the 50 normal cases.
